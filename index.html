Glossary of Marketing & Econometric Terms

Plain-English definitions, everyday analogies, and real-world examples for non-technical stakeholders.

1. Adstock (Ad Memory Decay) Memory Effect
Advertising impact does not vanish immediately when an ad campaign stops. A percentage of the ad message carries over into customer memory across future weeks and months.
☕ The Coffee Analogy: Drinking a cup of coffee at 8 AM makes you 100% awake. At 12 PM, you are still 50% awake because caffeine decays slowly in your body over time.
2. Log-Saturation (Diminishing Returns) Non-Linear Response
As you spend more money on the same ad channel, every extra dollar generates less additional sales lift because you run out of new, high-intent audiences to reach.
🍕 The Pizza Slice Analogy: When starving, the 1st pizza slice tastes amazing (High ROI). By the 10th slice, you are full; eating another slice gives zero extra enjoyment (Zero ROI).
3. Marginal ROI vs. Total ROI Next-Dollar Return
Total ROI is historical average return (Total Revenue / Total Spend). Marginal ROI measures how much revenue the NEXT $1 spend will generate right now ($7.07 Radio vs $1.32 Digital).
💡 Decision Rule: Always put your next marketing dollar into the channel with the highest Marginal ROI, even if another channel has higher total volume!
4. Standardised Coefficient (β*) Driver Importance
Scales all variables (digital millions, TV thousands, GDP percentages, COVID 0/1 toggles) onto a standard scale from -1 to +1 so we can compare driver importance fairly.
🍎 Comparing Apples to Elephants: Standardising converts all different units into standard deviations so you can directly compare which driver is truly strongest.
5. P-Value & Statistical Significance Confidence Test
The P-value tests whether an observed result was caused by real underlying impact or pure random luck. A $p < 0.05$ means less than a 5% chance of random luck (statistically significant!).
🪙 The Coin Flip Test: Flipping heads 10 times in a row has $p < 0.001$. It proves the coin is biased (real effect), not luck!
6. LOOCV R-Squared ($R^2$) Out-of-Sample Test
Leave-One-Out Cross Validation hides 1 month of sales data, trains the model on the remaining 35 months, and tests if it accurately predicts the hidden month.
🎓 Exam Score vs Practice Score: Regular $R^2$ (89.5%) is your practice test score. LOOCV $R^2$ (69.7%) is your final exam score on unseen questions, proving zero overfitting!
7. HC3 Robust Standard Errors Small-Sample Safety
A mathematical correction method used when working with small datasets ($n=36$ months) to prevent false-positive claims caused by unequal error variance (heteroscedasticity).
🛡️ Statistical Safety Net: Ensures that p-values are conservative and reliable so leadership never makes costly decisions based on statistical noise.
8. SciPy SLSQP Optimiser Smart Allocator
Sequential Least Squares Programming algorithm that automatically searches millions of budget combinations to find the exact mix that maximizes total sales under budget constraints.
🎯 Perfect Budget Reallocator: Found that shifting budget to 54% Digital / 46% Radio unlocks **+31.7% more revenue** for zero extra cost!
